# P6: OAuth 2, OpenID Connect, sessions and the scope model

The design and the plan for P6, written 2026-11-04 from `docs/architecture.md` ("Applications",
"Single sign-on", "Two-factor authentication", "OAuth profile", "HTTP surface"), the stack
document (`../dagda/TODO/2026-11-03-stack.md`, "Scope"), Dannan's `contract/scope.h`, Lir's
constraints for topic scopes (the Lir session, 2026-11-04), and the code as it stands after
the alignment. P6 is built in callback style on the frozen store, as the maintainer decided; nothing
in it is to be redone for Dagda except what "On Dagda" says in each section.

Each statement is **Settled** (the maintainer's decision, with where it is written) or **Proposed**.
What waits on the maintainer is collected at the end. Nothing is built yet.

## What P6 delivers

- The scope model: what a scope string is, who grants one to whom, how a principal's
  scopes are computed, and the grammar Lir waits on for MQTT topic permissions.
- Applications: registration, allowed groups, role mapping, client authentication.
- The Lugh session and the server-rendered sign-in: password, second factor, enrolment
  from a link, root's first sign-in, consent, the device-approval page.
- The grants: authorisation code with PKCE, refresh tokens with rotation, the device
  grant, pushed authorisation requests, client credentials.
- Tokens: signing keys and rotation, JWT access tokens, ID tokens, opaque handles, DPoP.
- The endpoints: discovery, JWKS, authorise, PAR, token, introspection, revocation,
  device, userinfo, end-session, back-channel logout.
- The API opened to callers: sessions with a CSRF token, bearer and DPoP tokens, and the
  routes for applications, scope grants, sessions and app passwords.
- From the LDAP plan, folded in: L1 (`given_name`, `family_name`, the POSIX number) and
  L2 (app passwords with a scope set).

Not in P6: WebAuthn and `phr` (P11; P6 refuses an app that requires `phr`), the dashboard
(P7), `lugh_client` (P10), dynamic client registration (never), front-channel logout
(never), SAML (never).

## The scope model

### Scope strings (Settled where marked; the rest Proposed)

A scope is a string without spaces, as RFC 6749 section 3.3 has it. Lugh knows four kinds:

| Kind | Form | Examples | Granted by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lugh's own | `lugh:<area>.<verb>` | `lugh:directory.read`, `lugh:credentials.write` (Settled: `lugh/api/scopes.h`, P5) | Scope grants to groups |
| OIDC | the standard names | `openid`, `profile`, `email`, `groups`, `offline_access` | Any person, by asking; what an app may ask for is in its registration |
| An application's | `<app>:<name>` | `toms:dns`, `macha:media.read` | Scope grants to groups; the app that owns `<app>` declares them |
| Lir's topic scopes | `lir:<action>:<filter>` | `lir:sub:devices/%7Bsub%7D/%23` | Scope grants to groups, as any application's |

`<app>` is the application's **scope prefix**, a short name chosen at registration, unique,
lower case, never changed (a `client_id` is random and long, and a scope must read). An
application of kind `api` declares the scopes it understands under its prefix; a token's
`scope` claim carries only scopes whose prefix matches one of the token's audiences, plus
the OIDC names, so a scope for one API never reaches another.

### Grants: groups hold scopes (Proposed)

A `scope_grants` table maps a group to the scopes its members hold, Lugh's own and any
application's. A principal's **effective scopes** are the union over its groups, read at
each sign-in, token issue and refresh, so removing someone from a group takes effect at
the next token and at once for their session's API calls. There is no per-user grant: a
person who needs a scope alone is a group of one. Genesis grants `administrators` every
Lugh scope; nothing else is granted until an administrator says so.

Service users (`Kind::service`) are principals as people are, in groups, so the same rule
computes their scopes when they bind with an app password. An application that
authenticates as itself (client credentials) is granted its scopes directly (Settled, the maintainer
2026-11-04): `app_scopes`, keyed by `client_id`, set at registration and changed through
the app's own route; its tokens carry `sub` = `client_id`, no `acr` or `amr`, and the
`Caller` kind `service`. Two grant tables, one rule each: groups for principals, the
registration for applications.

API: `GET /api/v1/scopes` (every scope Lugh and the registered APIs know), `GET`/`PUT` on
`/api/v1/groups/{id}/scopes` (`lugh:policies.write`: the whole set, written with
`If-Match`) and `PUT /api/v1/apps/{client_id}/scopes` (`lugh:apps.write`), each change
audited.

**LDAP's finding 2** is answered here: a main-password bind's scopes are its user's
effective scopes, and an app password's are its own set cut to them.

### What a token carries

Requested scopes are cut three times: to what the application may ask for (its
registration), to what the principal holds (the grants), and to what the token's
audiences can use (the prefix rule). A request for a scope the app may not ask for is
`invalid_scope`; a scope the principal does not hold is dropped silently and the issued
`scope` says what was granted, as RFC 6749 section 5.1 provides, so a client learns what
it has from the answer, not from the request.

### Topic scopes, for Lir (Proposed, with Lir's constraints of 2026-11-04 taken in)

The maintainer (via Lir, 2026-11-04): MQTT topic permissions are scopes in the Lugh token, checked
through Dannan's scope check; Lir keeps no ACL. The grammar:

```
topic-scope = "lir:" action ":" filter
action      = "pub" | "sub" | "retain"
filter      = an MQTT topic filter (MQTT 5.0 section 4.7), percent-encoded
```

- **Actions.** `pub`: PUBLISH without the retain flag to a topic the filter matches.
  `sub`: SUBSCRIBE with a filter the granted filter covers (subsumption, Lir's rule).
  `retain`: PUBLISH with the retain flag to a topic the filter matches; it does not imply
  `pub`. Lir asked for retain as optional; it is cheap in the grammar and lets a group
  publish state without being able to pin it.
- **Encoding.** The filter is UTF-8, percent-encoded as RFC 3986 section 2.1 for every
  byte that is a space, `%`, `{`, `}`, a control character (U+0000 to U+001F, U+007F) or
  outside ASCII; `/`, `+`, `#`, `$` and the rest of printable ASCII are literal. Decoding
  is one pass, done by Lir once at CONNECT. A scope whose decoded filter is not a valid
  MQTT filter (U+0000, `#` not last, `+` not a whole level) is refused by Lugh when the
  grant is written, never issued.
- **Substitution.** `{sub}` in a filter is replaced by the token's `sub`, as one literal
  level: a value containing `/`, `+` or `#` makes the scope match nothing. A literal brace
  is `%7B` or `%7D`, so an unencoded brace is always a substitution. Nothing else is
  substituted: `{client}` was in the draft and is dropped (Settled, the maintainer 2026-11-04, on
  Lir's review), because an MQTT client identifier is chosen by the client, so one
  principal could take another's and its grants. A device that needs its own grants is its
  own service principal, and `{sub}` names it.
- **`$` topics.** By MQTT's rule a filter starting with `#` or `+` never covers a topic
  starting with `$`; `$lir/`, `$dannan/` and `$macha/` are granted explicitly, for
  example `lir:sub:$lir/%23`. Lugh adds no rule of its own.
- **Shared subscriptions.** Lir checks the filter inside `$share/<group>/<filter>`
  against the `sub` grants; the grammar carries nothing for them.
- **Self-contained.** A topic scope needs no Lugh call to evaluate: Lir parses the set into
  a trie once per CONNECT or re-authentication.
- **Audience.** Lir is registered as an application of kind `api` with scope prefix `lir`
  and audience `lir`; a token for another audience carries no `lir:` scope and is refused
  by Lir on `aud`.
- **Size.** Lugh refuses to issue a token whose compact serialisation exceeds 8 KiB
  (`invalid_scope`, naming the size), and warns in the audit entry above 4 KiB. A group
  with many topic grants is the cause; wildcards and `{sub}` are the cure.
- **Lifetime and re-authentication.** Access tokens for Lir default to 15 minutes; Lir
  disconnects at `exp` unless an MQTT 5 re-authentication (method `lugh`) has brought a
  fresh token. On re-authentication with a narrower set, Lir drops the subscriptions the
  new set no longer covers (Settled, the maintainer 2026-11-04), by Lir's mechanism: MQTT cannot
  unsubscribe on a client's behalf without the client knowing, so Lir removes the
  uncovered subscriptions from the session and sends DISCONNECT 0x87; the client
  reconnects, resubscribes, and sees SUBACK 0x87 for what it lost. For 3.1 and 3.1.1
  clients, which cannot re-authenticate in band, this applies at reconnect.
- **JWKS without addressing Lugh.** Until P8, Lir fetches `/oauth2/jwks` over HTTPS and
  caches it by `kid`. On P8 (Settled, the maintainer 2026-11-04), Lugh publishes its signing public
  keys as retained values in `lugh/scope`, which every node holds, so any hosted system
  verifies a token offline from Dagda and never addresses Lugh. The retained-values part
  is Dagda's step 4.

Lir checks topic scopes itself, in its own trie, from the token (the maintainer, 2026-11-04, via Lir):
Dannan's `ScopeSource` and `Identity::verify` are the seam for writes under a prefix and
are not used for MQTT permissions. Lugh's part is the grammar, the grants and the token.

### The scope seam, on P8 (Settled: the stack document; the shape Proposed)

Lugh implements `dannan::contract::ScopeSource` over an in-memory image of `lugh/scope`
(principals, memberships, grants), refreshed from the head, and installs it with
`context.identity().install_scope_source(...)`. `may_write(principal, prefix)` answers
from memory with the generation it read. Until P8 nothing installs it, since Lugh is one
node on its own store; P6 writes the image and the answer, and P8 connects them. The
mapping from a prefix to a scope is Lugh's: `lugh/scope` and `lugh/secrets` are written by
node principals and by directory principals holding `lugh:directory.write` or
`lugh:credentials.write` respectively; other systems' prefixes map to that system's scopes
by its registration (`<app>:` prefix to `<app>:*` holders), and a prefix nobody declared
is refused.

## Applications

### Registration (Settled: architecture.md "What an application has"; the table Proposed)

The `apps` table, one record per application, keyed by `client_id` (random, `app_` and 22
base64url characters, never reused). Fields as the architecture lists them: name, icon and
home URL; kind (`web`, `spa`, `native`, `service`, `api`); scope prefix; client
authentication method and its material (the public key for `private_key_jwt`, the
certificate's subject or `x5t#S256` for `tls_client_auth`, the Argon2id hash of a secret for
`client_secret_basic`, which policy must allow); redirect URIs, post-logout URIs and the
back-channel logout URI; allowed grants; scopes the app may ask for; audiences; the
scopes the app itself holds (`app_scopes`), for one that calls as itself; required `acr`
and `max_age`; first
party; sender constraint (`dpop`, `mtls`, `none`); token lifetimes; state. `app_access`
is a second table, `<client_id>/<group_id>`, listing the groups whose members may sign
in; role mapping is a third, `<client_id>/<group_id>`, carrying the role names the group
maps to for that app's audience.

API, all `lugh:apps.write` (a new scope, held by `administrators` at genesis) and
`lugh:directory.read` for reading: `GET`/`POST /api/v1/apps`, `GET`/`PATCH`/`DELETE
/api/v1/apps/{client_id}` with `If-Match`, `PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/apps/{client_id}/access/{group}`,
`PUT /api/v1/apps/{client_id}/roles/{group}` (`{roles: [...]}`), `POST
/api/v1/apps/{client_id}/secret` (a new secret, shown once, the old one valid for an
overlap of 24 hours). Every change is audited with the before-and-after diff the dashboard
will show. Disabling an app revokes every grant and session it holds (the tombstone rule).

### Rules (Settled: architecture.md "Rules")

Only an administrator registers an app; an unknown `client_id`, a disabled app or an
unmatched `redirect_uri` gets an error page on Lugh and never a redirect; `sub` is stable
for ever; first-party apps skip consent.

## The session and the sign-in

### The Lugh session (Settled: `__Host-` cookie, replicated, the maintainer 2026-11-04; the record Proposed)

A session is a record in `sessions`, keyed by the SHA-256 of a 256-bit random handle, so
the table holds nothing that works as a cookie. The cookie is `__Host-lugh_session`,
`Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/`. The record: the user, `sid` (a second random
value the ID tokens carry, so a token never reveals the handle), `acr`, `amr`,
`auth_time`, created, last seen, the idle and absolute lifetimes (Settled, the maintainer
2026-11-04: 24 hours idle, 30 days absolute by default, both configurable), the CSRF secret, and the applications signed into
through it with each app's `sid` use, for back-channel logout. Every API call through a
session carries `X-CSRF-Token`, the HMAC of the session handle under the CSRF secret, and
a request without it is `403`. On Dagda: in `lugh/secrets`, replicated, so a session works
at any Lugh node; the merge keeps a revocation over anything.

A sign-in in progress is a **pre-session**: the `SignIn` state the authenticator already
hands back, kept server side under the cookie `__Host-lugh_signin` (same flags, lifetime
15 minutes, the authenticator's `lifetime`), node-local, and gone when the sign-in ends
either way. On Dagda it stays node-local: a sign-in that started on one node finishes
there, and a request that reaches another node with a pre-session it does not have starts
again at the password.

### The pages (Settled: server-rendered, no JavaScript beyond WebAuthn later)

`/signin` (password), `/signin/factor` (TOTP or a recovery code), `/signin/enrol/{token}`
(set a password from an enrolment link), `/signin/enrol/totp` (the secret, as a QR code
rendered server side as SVG, and the code back), `/signin/enrol/recovery` (the codes,
shown once), `/signin/consent`, `/signin/device` (type the user code, then approve),
`/signin/done`, `/signout`. Each is an HTML template compiled into the binary from
`lugh/signin/templates/`, in Lugh's styling (the dashboard's tokens, plain CSS, served from
the binary), with a strict CSP (`default-src 'none'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self'
data:; form-action 'self'`). Every form carries a CSRF token bound to the pre-session.
Pages are rate limited by client address as open routes are, and the authenticator's
back-off answers before any hashing. Root's first sign-in goes from the password to
`/signin/enrol/totp` by the genesis allowance, once.

A sign-in is reached in one of two ways: directly, at `/signin`, which ends at
`/signin/done` with the session set; or from `/oauth2/authorize`, which stores the
authorisation request in the pre-session, runs the sign-in and consent, and ends with
the redirect carrying the code.

### Step-up and policy (Settled: architecture.md "Policy")

Policy is the strictest of global, the user's groups and the app (`Policies::required`
grows an app argument). An app's `acr_values` or `max_age`, or its registered minimum, that
the session does not meet sends the user through exactly the missing step; an app that
requires `phr` is refused with `unmet_authentication_requirements` (OIDC Core 3.1.2.6)
until P11. `prompt=login` forces the password; `prompt=none` answers `login_required`
without a page.

### What a session change does (Settled: architecture.md "Signing out and revocation")

Signing out at Lugh, or an app's end-session call, removes the session, sends a
back-channel logout token (a signed JWT with `sid` and `events`) to every app the session
signed into, and revokes the refresh tokens issued under it. Disabling a user, removing
them from an app's groups, resetting their factors, or a password change by its owner does
the same for every session of theirs. A password change by its owner (`POST
/api/v1/me/password`, session only, with the old password) arrives here, as the directory
and LDAP plans expect.

## Grants (Settled: the list; the single-use rule Settled, its shape Proposed, open question 2)

The `grants` table already holds enrolment links, keyed by the SHA-256 of the token. P6
adds, with the same keying: authorisation codes (lifetime 60 seconds, bound to the client,
redirect URI, PKCE challenge, nonce, scopes, session and `acr`), PAR request URIs
(`urn:ietf:params:oauth:request_uri:` and the handle, 60 seconds), device codes (the device
code and the user code, 10 minutes, polled at the interval given, `slow_down` when faster),
refresh tokens (a family ID, the session, the client, the scopes, idle and absolute
lifetimes), and consents (`consents`: user, client, scopes, when).

Every one of these is single use. **The design for open question 2:**

- A code, a PAR request URI and a device code are **node-local**: the handle's first
  segment is the issuing node's ID, the record lives in that node's space, and a node that
  receives one it does not own forwards the redemption to the owner over Dannan (message
  type 512, `redeem`), fails closed if the owner is unreachable, and never redeems it
  itself. They live seconds to minutes, so the loss of a node loses only sign-ins in
  flight, which start again.
- A refresh token is a longer-lived grant and the one place this hurts: the app's
  `RelyingParty` refreshes every 15 minutes and ends its session when a refresh fails, so
  with node-local refresh tokens the loss of a node signs out every user it issued one
  to. So refresh tokens are **replicated with the session** (Settled, the maintainer 2026-11-04) in
  `lugh/secrets`, and their single use is enforced by the version of the family record,
  as the TOTP step is by its record's version: a rotation writes the family at the
  version it read, and the second of two concurrent rotations loses and is treated as
  reuse, which revokes the family. On a partition the two sides can each rotate once, and
  the merge keeps the newest version and revokes the sessions of the loser, as the
  credential rule already says. That is the bound accepted for sessions; a refresh token
  is part of its session.
- Until P8 the store is one node's, and all of this is the same code with one node.

Reuse of a rotated refresh token revokes the whole family and the session's apps are
told (RFC 9700 section 4.14). A consent is not single use; it is the record a later
sign-in reads to skip the page.

## Tokens

### Signing keys (Settled: architecture.md "OAuth profile"; the table Proposed)

`signing_keys`: one record per key, sealed, with `kid` (random), algorithm (`ES256` by
default; `EdDSA` where an app asks for it), the private key as `SecureBuffer`, created,
not-before, and retired-at. A new key is written and published in JWKS before anything is
signed with it (`not_before` a configurable lead, default 1 hour), and the old one is
retired after every token signed with it has expired. Rotation is `POST
/api/v1/keys/rotate` (`lugh:keys.write`, administrators) and a scheduled default of 90
days. The private keys are held in Dannan's locked memory and signed with Dannan's
`identity::crypto` (ES256 and Ed25519); Lugh adds only the JWS and JWT framing, in
`lugh/tokens/`, with a fuzz target on the parser. On Dagda the keys are records in
`lugh/secrets` and the public halves retained values in `lugh/scope`.

### Access tokens (Settled: JWT by default, RFC 9068; opaque per client)

Claims: `iss` (Lugh's issuer URL, open question 14), `sub` (the principal's ID), `aud` (the
app's audiences), `client_id`, `exp`, `iat`, `nbf`, `jti`, `scope`, `acr`, `amr`,
`auth_time`, `sid`, `roles` (the app's role mapping for the user's groups, for its own
audience only), `groups` only when the `groups` scope was granted (group names), and
`cnf` with `jkt` (DPoP) or `x5t#S256` (mTLS) when sender-constrained. Default lifetime 5
minutes, per app. Header: `typ: at+jwt`, `alg`, `kid`. An app registered for opaque
tokens gets a random handle instead, with the same claims kept in `grants` and read by
introspection.

### ID tokens (Settled: OIDC Core)

`iss`, `sub`, `aud` (the `client_id`), `exp`, `iat`, `auth_time`, `nonce`, `acr`, `amr`,
`azp`, `sid`, `at_hash`, `c_hash` where applicable, and the claims the granted scopes
allow: `profile` gives `name`, `given_name`, `family_name`, `preferred_username` (the
user's name) and `updated_at`; `email` gives `email`; `groups` gives `groups`. Userinfo
answers the same claims for the bearer of an access token with `openid`.

### Sender constraint (Settled: DPoP and mTLS, both in P6, the maintainer 2026-11-04)

DPoP (RFC 9449): the proof is verified on the token endpoint and, by the resource (the
API), on every request, with a 5-minute `jti` replay window per node, `nonce` from the
server when it chooses, and `ath` on resource requests. `tls_client_auth` (RFC 8705
section 2.1, the certificate's subject DN or `x5t#S256` as registered) and mTLS binding
(`cnf.x5t#S256`, checked by the API against the connection's certificate): `TlsContext`
gains a mode that requests, and verifies against the operator's client CA, a client
certificate without requiring one (`verify_peer` without `fail_if_no_peer_cert`), so one
listener serves browsers and certificated clients; the certificate's thumbprint reaches
the router in `RouteContext` as the client address does. Introspection also accepts
`tls_client_auth` from `api` apps.

## The endpoints (Settled: architecture.md "HTTP surface")

| Endpoint | Does |
|---|---|
| `GET /.well-known/openid-configuration`, `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server` | Discovery (RFC 8414, OIDC Discovery): the endpoints, the algorithms, `code_challenge_methods_supported: ["S256"]`, `require_pushed_authorization_requests` per policy, `dpop_signing_alg_values_supported`, `backchannel_logout_supported` |
| `GET /oauth2/jwks` | The public keys, `Cache-Control: max-age=300` |
| `GET /oauth2/authorize` | `response_type=code` only, PKCE S256 required, `redirect_uri` exact, `state` required, `nonce` required with `openid`; `request_uri` from PAR; into the sign-in |
| `POST /oauth2/par` | RFC 9126: the request stored, a `request_uri` answered; required for confidential clients when policy says |
| `POST /oauth2/token` | `authorization_code`, `refresh_token`, `client_credentials`, `urn:ietf:params:oauth:grant-type:device_code`; client authentication as registered; DPoP |
| `POST /oauth2/device` | RFC 8628: `device_code`, `user_code` (8 characters from a 20-letter alphabet, shown as `XXXX-XXXX`), `verification_uri` (`/signin/device`), `verification_uri_complete`, `interval` |
| `POST /oauth2/introspect` | RFC 7662, for registered `api` apps authenticating as clients: active or not, and the claims |
| `POST /oauth2/revoke` | RFC 7009: a refresh token (and its family) or an access token (by `jti`, kept in a per-node deny list until `exp`) |
| `GET`/`POST /oauth2/userinfo` | The claims the token's scopes allow |
| `GET /oauth2/logout` | RP-initiated logout (OIDC RP-Initiated Logout 1.0): `id_token_hint`, `post_logout_redirect_uri` exact, `state`; ends the session, sends back-channel logout |
| (outbound) back-channel logout | `POST` of a logout token to each app's registered URI, retried with back-off for an hour, audited |

Errors follow RFC 6749 section 5.2 and OIDC Core 3.1.2.6, as JSON on the token endpoints
and as a redirect with `error` and `state` on the authorisation endpoint only when the
`redirect_uri` and client were valid; otherwise an error page on Lugh.

## The API opens

`CallerResolver` (`lugh/http/caller.h`) gets its first implementation, `lugh/authn/callers.h`:

- A session cookie with a valid `X-CSRF-Token`: a `Caller` of kind `user`, scopes the
  user's effective scopes, `acr` carried for routes that need `phr` later.
- `Authorization: Bearer <jwt>`: verified offline against the current keys, `aud` must
  include `lugh` (the API is itself an `api` app with prefix `lugh`), scopes from the
  token; kind `user` or `service` by the principal (`Caller::Kind` gains `service`, as
  blackraven-admin's plan asked).
- `Authorization: DPoP <jwt>` with the `DPoP` header: as bearer, plus the proof.
- Opaque handles: read from `grants`; the resolver stays in memory by holding a bounded
  cache of handles seen, invalidated on revocation. Proposed: opaque tokens are not
  accepted by `/api/v1` in P6, only by `introspect`; a JWT is required there.

The router's "answered from memory" rule holds: the resolver reads keys and the session
cache from memory, and a session's record is read through the route's own deadline by the
route (the resolver resolves the handle to the cached session, the route refreshes "last
seen" at most once a minute).

New scopes: `lugh:apps.write`, `lugh:keys.write`, `lugh:sessions.write` (revoking others'
sessions). `GET /api/v1/me` (the caller), `GET`/`DELETE /api/v1/me/sessions[/{sid}]`,
`POST /api/v1/me/password`, `GET`/`POST`/`DELETE /api/v1/me/app-passwords` (L2's
self-service), and `GET`/`DELETE /api/v1/users/{id}/sessions` for administrators.

## Data

Tables added or filled: `apps`, `app_access`, `app_roles`, `consents`, `sessions`,
`grants` (shared with enrolment links), `signing_keys`, `scope_grants`, `app_passwords`
(L2), and `User` grows `given_name`, `family_name` and the POSIX number (L1). Merge rules
on Dagda are the architecture's: sessions and refresh families versioned with the
credential rule; codes, PAR and device codes node-local; revocations, disabled apps and
retired keys tombstones; `scope_grants` ordinary attributes in `lugh/scope`.

## Not built in P6, and why

- WebAuthn, `phr`: P11. An app requiring `phr` is refused until then.
- Opaque access tokens at `/api/v1`: JWT only there; opaque per client for third parties.
- Dynamic client registration, front-channel logout, the implicit and password grants,
  SAML, federation to upstream providers: as the architecture says, never or not planned.
- `lugh_client`: P10, written from `docs/oauth.md` once P6 has fixed the surface.

## Decided (the maintainer, 2026-11-04)

Through AskUserQuestion, with Lir's review of the topic-scope draft taken in:

1. **Refresh tokens** are replicated with the session, single use by the family's version;
   the partition bound is the one accepted for sessions.
2. **Narrowing on re-authentication:** Lir drops the subscriptions the new set no longer
   covers, by removing them and sending DISCONNECT 0x87 so the client learns of it.
3. **JWKS on P8:** the signing public keys as retained values in `lugh/scope`; HTTPS JWKS
   until then.
4. **mTLS** client authentication and token binding are in P6 (O8).
5. **Client-credentials apps** are granted scopes directly (`app_scopes`); `sub` is the
   `client_id`.
6. **Session lifetimes:** 24 hours idle, 30 days absolute by default, configurable.
7. **OIDC from the start**; open question 3 is closed.
8. **`{client}` is dropped** from the topic-scope grammar; `{sub}` alone is substituted.
9. **Lir checks topic scopes itself**, not through Dannan's scope seam (the maintainer, via Lir).
